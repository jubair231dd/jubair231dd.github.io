About

I'm a biostatistician and data scientist who uses machine learning and rigorous statistical methods to answer questions in health and clinical research.

Most recently, I worked as a Research Associate/Biostatistician at the Harvard School of Dental Medicine, where I provided quantitative and statistical support for clinical research on oral health outcomes and developed machine learning models to predict post‑operative dental pain trajectories from longitudinal clinical trial data.

I hold an M.S. in Computer Science (Data Science) from the University of Tennessee at Chattanooga, where my research focused on estimating the transmission fitness advantage of SARS‑CoV‑2 variants. Before that, I completed a B.S. in Civil Engineering at BUET, with a thesis on geospatial analysis of COVID‑19 spread using GIS and remote sensing.

Across these projects, my focus has stayed consistent: using data‑driven, statistically rigorous methods to answer questions that matter for public health.

Selected Research Projects

Genomics

Phylogenetic Tree: Agglomerative Clustering to Understand Omicron Sub‑lineage Evolution

  • Analyzed the evolution of co‑circulating SARS‑CoV‑2 variants to identify predominantly affected regions, using the neighbor‑joining algorithm to map Omicron sub‑variant fitness growth dynamics.

Statistics

Unraveling the Factors Contributing to Variability in Health Insurance Charges in the US

  • Conducted EDA, hypothesis testing, and multiple linear regression on a US healthcare insurance dataset (1,338 records) to understand pricing determinants.
  • Found Age, BMI, and Smoking to be statistically significant predictors of premiums, supporting evidence‑based insurance pricing strategies.

Deep Learning

Augmenting High‑Dimensional Gene Expression Data Using GANs

  • Used Generative Adversarial Networks to generate synthetic gene expression data, addressing sample size limitations in biological datasets.
  • Trained Random Forest and KNN classifiers on original, GAN‑augmented, and synthetic‑only datasets; applied PCA to assess similarity between real and generated data.
  • Achieved up to 70.4% (RF) and 63.9% (KNN) accuracy with combined data, versus 28.5% (RF) and 27.3% (KNN) with synthetic‑only data.

Evaluating the Impact of Medicaid Unwinding on Children's Oral Health Access in the United States

Investigating the effects of post‑2023 Medicaid disenrollment on children's access to dental care using nationally representative survey data. Developing ML and deep learning models — Random Forest, CatBoost, Ensemble Learning, and gradient boosting — to predict non‑resolving pain group membership. Also built an automated data quality monitoring workflow for large‑scale survey data.
